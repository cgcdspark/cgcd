## COLD OPEN (≈0:00–0:55) **[SHORT 1: "How Jensen Huang lost Nvidia in four days"]**

🎬 Black. A cursor blinks on a dark email draft. 🔊 One low drone. A single keyboard tap.

> On Sunday, November the eighth, Jensen Huang sent an email to everyone at Nvidia.
> It started with an apology.
> ⏸
> *"I was unfaithful to my wife."*

🎬 Quick cuts: Nvidia HQ exterior · trading screen turning red · the 8-K page, stylised. 🔊 Pulse begins.

> Four days later, Nvidia told the world he was gone. Not just as CEO. Off the board too. Effective immediately.
> Think about that. For thirty-three years, Nvidia had one boss.
> The guy who started it in a Denny's booth. Who once had it thirty days from going under… and turned it into the first company in history worth five trillion dollars.
> Gone. In less than a week.

🔊 Music drops out. ⏸

> But the affair… wasn't really the story.
> Behind it was a businesswoman, a company in Singapore, billions of dollars of AI chips… and questions that now reach all the way to the US Justice Department and Congress.
> So what actually happened?
> And what happens to Nvidia now?

🎬 **TITLE: NVIDIA AFTER JENSEN.** 🔊 Hit, then a riser.

## CHAPTER 1 — THE DINER (≈0:55–2:15) **[SHORT 2: "Nvidia was 30 days from going bust"]**

🎬 Archive-style stills: 1970s Taiwan street · Kentucky countryside · a Denny's sign at night (generic). 🔊 Lighter, driving underscore.

> To understand what Nvidia just lost, you have to go back.
> He was nine when his family sent him to America. His relatives thought they'd found him a good boarding school in Kentucky. It turned out to be a school for troubled kids. One of his jobs? Cleaning the toilets.
> A few years later, he was washing dishes at Denny's.
> And in 1993, in a Denny's booth in East San Jose, he and two engineer friends, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, sketched out a company that would build graphics chips.

🎬 Original graphic: a 1993 → 2026 timeline that draws itself across the screen.

> It almost died straight away.
> Their first chip bet on the wrong technology. The whole industry went one way; Nvidia went the other. By 1996, more than half the staff had been let go.
> One customer, Sega, dropped Nvidia's chip… and then invested five million dollars anyway. Jensen says that gave them six months to live.
> The company even had an unofficial motto: *"Our company is thirty days from going out of business."*
> ⏸
> They survived. In 1999, Nvidia went public at twelve dollars a share.
> And sitting on its board that year was a forty-five-year-old director called Harvey Jones.
> Remember that name.

## CHAPTER 2 — THE BET (≈2:15–3:40) **[SHORT 3: "The bet Wall Street hated"]**

🎬 Macro chip footage · code on screen · graphic: market-value line diving in 2008.

> Then Jensen made the bet that defined him.
> In 2006, Nvidia launched CUDA, software that turned its gaming chips into machines that could do almost any kind of maths.
> It cost a fortune. Wall Street didn't get it. By late 2008, the whole company was worth less than four billion dollars.
> And then, in 2012, a grad student trained an AI program called AlexNet on two Nvidia gaming cards. About five hundred dollars each. In his bedroom, at his parents' house.
> It blew away the competition. Nvidia's own annual report calls that moment AI's "Big Bang".

🔊 Swell.

> In 2016, Jensen personally delivered Nvidia's first AI supercomputer to a small non-profit called OpenAI.
> Six years later, OpenAI released ChatGPT.
> And suddenly, everyone needed Nvidia.
> It touched a trillion dollars in 2023. And in October 2025 it became the first company ever worth five trillion.

## CHAPTER 3 — THE EMPIRE (≈3:40–5:00) **[SHORT 4: "Nvidia's profit in ONE quarter"]**

🎬 Data-centre footage. Graphic: big counter **$115.8bn** → **$65.3bn profit**.

> Here's how big it got.
> In the three months to late October, Nvidia sold almost a hundred and sixteen billion dollars' worth of products. Around nine in every ten of those dollars came from data centres.
> And it kept sixty-five billion of it as profit. In one quarter.
> Jensen ran the whole thing like nobody else. Dozens of people reporting straight to him. The same leather jacket on every stage.

🔊 Music thins. A clock starts ticking, quietly.

> But there was one market Nvidia could never fully have. China.
> Not long ago, China was about one dollar in every five Nvidia made. Then US export rules cut off its most powerful chips. Today, Nvidia's own forecast assumes zero data-centre chip sales there.
> And that's exactly where this story takes a turn.

## CHAPTER 4 — THE SINGAPORE CONNECTION (≈5:00–6:30) **[SHORT 5: "The Singapore company at the centre of the Nvidia scandal"]**

🎬 Graphic: map of China, Singapore, Malaysia and Taiwan, with lines drawing between them. Singapore skyline at night. 🔊 Radio static under a low pulse.

> Meet Megaspeed. A data-centre company based in Singapore.
> According to reports, it became one of Nvidia's biggest customers in South-East Asia, buying billions of dollars' worth of its chips.
> In 2024, investigators at the US Commerce Department noticed what they later called "suspicious activity". They started looking at whether chips bought by Megaspeed were ending up in China.
> By mid-2025, Nvidia had stopped selling to Megaspeed.
> That June, US and Singapore officials visited the company and interviewed its boss: a businesswoman who, according to Bloomberg, had spent years in Nvidia's orbit.
> ⏸
> Today, that investigation is a criminal case, led by the US Justice Department.
> Megaspeed says it does legitimate, lawful business, and denies any wrongdoing.

## CHAPTER 5 — FIFTEEN DAYS (≈6:30–8:15) **[SHORT 6: "15 days that ended Jensen Huang's reign"]**

🎬 Case-file timeline board: each date stamps in with a typewriter clack. 🔊 Ticking clock, sub-bass pulse, a hit on each date.

> October the twenty-ninth, 2026.
> The New York Times reports that, according to US officials, American intelligence suspected that same businesswoman of working for Chinese intelligence.
> Nvidia hits back the same day. It says the allegations about chips reaching China were investigated and debunked last year, and that meeting customers is just part of a CEO's job.
> The next day, the Wall Street Journal reports that Singapore and Taiwan had concerns about her too. Singapore's government publicly denies saying that.
> Nvidia's shares start to slide.
> November the fourth. Three committees of the US House of Representatives write to Jensen Huang. They want answers.
> ⏸
> November the eighth. A Sunday. The email.
> *"I was unfaithful to my wife."*
> The same day, Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation after reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised.
> November the ninth. China's foreign ministry calls the spy claim "completely groundless". President Trump says Huang told him he had no idea, and that he believes him. A shareholder sues, claiming investors were misled. Those are allegations, not findings.
> November the eleventh. The New York Times reports that Huang also had a relationship with a senior Nvidia executive. Nvidia doesn't comment.
> That same day, the board accepts his resignation.
> 🔊 Everything cuts to silence. ⏸
> November the twelfth. The announcement.
> After thirty-three years… the only CEO Nvidia had ever had… was gone.
> Days later, Reuters reported a relationship with yet another Nvidia employee. Again, Nvidia declined to comment.

## CHAPTER 6 — WHAT WE ACTUALLY KNOW (≈8:15–9:45) **[SHORT 7: "Was it a honey trap? Here's what we actually know"]**

🎬 Graphic split: **ALLEGED** column / **ESTABLISHED** column. 🔊 Cold drone, heartbeat.

> So… was this a honey trap? That's what everyone's asking.
> Here's what we actually know.
> She hasn't been charged with anything, anywhere. She hasn't said a word in public. Bloomberg reports she's living in Beijing. China calls the whole thing a smear.
> The US Attorney General says the Justice Department has reached "no conclusions" about who she is. And President Trump put it bluntly: "nobody's proven that she's a spy."
> Nvidia's board says it has no reason to believe any secrets were compromised.
> ⏸
> But the investigations keep coming.
> The Justice Department's criminal case into Megaspeed. A reported SEC inquiry into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. Malaysia checking whether chips were shipped on to somewhere else.
> And the Treasury Secretary went on TV and asked whether Nvidia had made "seemingly illicit sales" to boost its numbers. Nvidia hasn't been charged with anything.
> On December the first, Jensen Huang has agreed to sit down, behind closed doors, with three House committees.
> And Jensen himself? He's posted that he'd be "honored" to come back.
> The board's answer: it does "not intend to reinstate" him.

## CHAPTER 7 — THE EMPTY CHAIR (≈9:45–10:55) **[SHORT 8: "Who's running Nvidia now?"]**

🎬 Empty stage under a spotlight (graphic or generic). Lower thirds: **Colette Kress, interim CEO** · **Harvey Jones, chair**. 🔊 Tension eases slightly; a steady pulse.

> So who's running the most important company in AI right now?
> Its finance chief, Colette Kress, is doing two jobs: interim CEO and CFO.
> And remember Harvey Jones? The director from 1999? He's been on the board since 1993. He's now Nvidia's first-ever chair. The man who watched Nvidia grow up is now picking who runs it next.
> The business? On November the seventeenth, Nvidia posted the biggest quarter in its history. Kress's message: *"Our momentum is real."* It's forecasting about a hundred and thirty billion dollars next quarter.
> But the stock tells a different story. At its October peak, Nvidia was worth nearly six trillion dollars. On earnings day, it was under four trillion, though part of that slide was a market-wide sell-off.
> And its biggest customers? Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta are all building chips of their own.

## CHAPTER 8 — WHAT HAPPENS NEXT (≈10:55–11:50) **[SHORT 9: "3 ways this ends for Nvidia"]**

🎬 Scenario graphic: three paths branching. 🔊 Build.

> So how does this end? Three ways it could play out.
> **One: business as usual.** Nvidia designs its chips years ahead. The roadmap already runs to 2028. A strong new CEO steps in and the machine keeps running. After Steve Jobs, Apple became the first US company worth a trillion dollars.
> **Two: the slow squeeze.** Customers keep building their own chips, China stays shut, and without its founder pushing, Nvidia's edge slowly wears down. After Bill Gates handed over the CEO job, Microsoft's stock took nearly seventeen years to beat its old high.
> **Three: the investigations go deeper.** If they find something serious about Megaspeed, export controls or what investors were told, that could hurt Nvidia far more than losing a CEO. If they find nothing, this becomes the story of one man's private life… and an empire that outlived him.
> Watch three things: who the board picks, what happens on December the first, and whether the biggest buyers keep ordering.
