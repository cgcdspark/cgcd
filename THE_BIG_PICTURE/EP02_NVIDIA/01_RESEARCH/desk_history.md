## 1. Early life (1963–1984)

- **Born 17 Feb 1963 in Taipei**, Taiwan. He moved to **Tainan** as a small child. (Wikipedia editors explicitly correct "born in Tainan" to Taipei.) He was the second son of Huang Hsing-tai, a chemical engineer at an oil refinery, and Lo Tsai-hsiu, a schoolteacher [H01]. His mother taught her sons English with ten random dictionary words a day (Kim pp.17–18; CNBC 2018) [H02].
- **Thailand at about age 5.** The family moved to Bangkok for his father's refinery work, for roughly four years. He attended Ruamrudee International School [H03].
- **USA, 1973, at age 9.** He and his older brother were sent to an uncle in Tacoma, Washington. The relatives enrolled the boys at the **Oneida Baptist Institute** in Oneida, Kentucky, believing it was a prestigious boarding school. It was in fact a strict religious academy that took troubled youth. His parents sold nearly everything to pay the tuition (Kim p.18) [H04]. Jensen was too young for the Institute's classes, so he went to the local public elementary school. Kim describes him there as "an undersized Asian immigrant with long hair and heavily accented English". He was bullied, and he cleaned the dorm toilets (Kim pp.18–19) [H05]. Huang's own later line, *paraphrased in reports of a 2024 Stanford appearance*, is that he cleaned more toilets than the whole audience combined [H06, partial]. In a 2002 *Wired* interview he said he remembered Kentucky "more vividly than just about any other" period of his life [H07].
- **Oregon.** About two years later his parents emigrated and settled in Beaverton. At Aloha High School he skipped two grades, graduated at 16, and was a nationally ranked table-tennis player (Kim p.19) [H08].
- **Denny's, 1978–1983, from age 15.** He worked as a dishwasher, busboy and waiter at a Portland-area Denny's [H09].
- **Oregon State University**, chosen for its low tuition (Kim p.21). He earned a **BSEE in 1984** with highest honors. There he met **Lori Mills**, his electrical-engineering lab partner. They married in **1985** and have two children [H10][H11].
- **AMD → LSI Logic.** NVIDIA's own 10-K gives **AMD 1983–1985** (microprocessor designer) and **LSI Logic 1985–1993** (rising to Director of CoreWare) [H12]. Wikipedia says AMD 1984–85, so **use the 10-K dates**. At LSI he worked with Sun Microsystems engineers **Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem** on the GX graphics engine (1989) (Kim pp.22–23, 33–37) [H13]. He earned a Stanford MSEE in 1992 while working [H14].

## 2. Founding (1993)

- **Denny's, Berryessa Road, East San Jose.** Through 1992 the three met at a Denny's booth to write the business plan. Huang picked the place because it was quieter than home and the coffee was cheap (paraphrase of Kim p.41 and 60 Minutes, 28 Apr 2024) [H15]. Denny's installed a plaque at the booth on **26 Sep 2023** marking the "birthplace of a $1 trillion company" [H16]. The plaque was **removed in Nov 2026** after the scandal (reported) [H17].
- **Incorporated 5 April 1993** in California. Primary sources: the 1999 prospectus's financial tables ("Inception (April 5, 1993)") and NVIDIA's corporate timeline [H18].
- **Initial capital, CONTESTED:**
  - **$600.** Per Kim (p.43), lawyer **James Gaither** of Cooley Godward capitalised the company with $200 of Huang's money, and Priem and Malachowsky then put in $200 each [H19]. (Gaither later sat on NVIDIA's board; he is listed as a director, age 61, in the 1999 prospectus [H20].)
  - **"$40,000 in the bank".** This figure appears in Wikipedia's Nvidia article, citing Fortune (Nusca, 16 Nov 2017) [H21].
  - Both figures may be true at different moments: share capital versus cash on hand. **Narration: "a few hundred dollars of share capital" is the tighter claim. If you use $40,000, attribute it to Fortune's 2017 account.**
- **The name.** "NVision" was taken, so the founders turned to Latin *invidia* (envy). Priem wanted competitors "green with envy" (Kim p.42) [H22].
- **Venture money.** LSI CEO **Wilf Corrigan** introduced Huang to **Don Valentine** of **Sequoia Capital** (Witt p.31). **Sutter Hill Ventures** also invested [H23]. Amount: Forbes (2002) says "$2 million from Sequoia Capital and others", while Wikipedia's text says $20 million. **Treat $2 million as the 1993 round. The $20m figure is unsupported by the cited source.** By 1999 both firms had partners on the board: Mark Stevens (Sequoia) and Tench Coxe (Sutter Hill) [H24].
- **CEO from day one at age 30.** Priem: "we basically deferred to Jensen on day one" (Kim p.55) [H25].

## 3. Near-death (1995–1997)

- **NV1, May 1995.** The 1999 prospectus states it plainly. The NV1 "was developed in the absence of industry standards". Then Microsoft's Direct3D and SGI's OpenGL won, so "the Company experienced a significant reduction in revenue from sales of the NV1 and stopped selling the NV1 in the first quarter of 1996". NVIDIA also "ceased development of the NV2, a product designed for a game console platform" [H26]. NV1 used quadrilateral primitives rather than the triangles the industry standardised on [H27].
- **Losses in the prospectus (primary):** net loss of $6.38m in 1995 and $3.08m in 1996. Accumulated deficit of about **$17.1m** by Oct 1998. All revenue in 1995–96 came from NV1 [H28].
- **Sega.** The NV2 was a Sega console chip, which became the Dreamcast project. The prospectus does not name Sega; it says only that NVIDIA "developed the NV2 under contract with a third party" and booked $2.0m (1995) and $3.0m (1996) as R&D credits [H29]. Sega president **Shoichiro Irimajiri** told Huang that Sega would use another vendor, yet persuaded Sega to pay/invest **$5 million** anyway (WSJ, Ben Cohen, 18 May 2024) [H30]. Huang told the story in his **NTU commencement speech, 27 May 2023**: Irimajiri's "understanding and generosity gave us six months to live". **WORDING CONTESTED:** Fortune's report renders it "six months to survive". Check the video [H31].
- **The 1996 layoffs.** Headcount fell from roughly **100 to 40** (Witt, New Yorker 2023; Jon Peddie 2019) [H32].
- **"Thirty days."** When the RIVA 128 shipped in **August 1997**, NVIDIA had about one month of payroll left. Out of that came the unofficial motto **"Our company is thirty days from going out of business,"** which Huang used to open staff presentations for years (Witt, *New Yorker*, 27 Nov 2023) [H33]. Huang's own later wording, *as reported* from **The Joe Rogan Experience (early Dec 2025)**: "You know the phrase '30 days from going out of business,' I've used for 33 years" (partial; reported by Business Insider, Entrepreneur and Fortune) [H34].
- **RIVA 128: the save.** The prospectus records commercial shipment in August 1997. Revenue jumped from **$3.9m (1996) to $29.1m (1997)** and then **$92.7m in the first nine months of FY1998** [H35]. About a million units sold in four months (Peddie) [H36]. Headcount recovered to **184** by Oct 1998, up from 71 a year earlier [H37].

## 4. IPO and "the world's first GPU" (1999)

- **IPO.** The 424B4 prospectus is dated **21 Jan 1999**, and trading began **22 Jan 1999** on Nasdaq. It offered **3,500,000 shares at $12.00** (about $42m gross), above the S-1/A's $9–11 range. Lead underwriter: Morgan Stanley [H38]. **Huang was 35** and is listed as "President, Chief Executive Officer and Director" [H39].
- **Hidden hook:** the same 1999 director list includes **Harvey C. Jones, Jr., age 45**, the man NVIDIA named its "first-ever Chair of the Board" on 12 Nov 2026 to steer the company after Huang [H40].
- **GeForce 256.** Announced 31 Aug 1999. Marketed as "the world's first 'GPU'". NVIDIA defined a GPU as "a single-chip processor with integrated transform, lighting, triangle setup/clipping, and rendering engines that is capable of processing a minimum of 10 million polygons per second" [H41]. The FY2000 10-K (filed 13 Mar 2000) says: "We are recognized for developing the world's first GPU". It also says commercial shipment began in **August 1999**, while Wikipedia gives a 11 Oct 1999 retail release, so **the date is contested** [H42]. **Framing caution:** "world's first GPU" was a *marketing definition* coined by NVIDIA. Earlier chips did 3D acceleration. Say "NVIDIA branded it the world's first GPU" [H41].
- **Scale-up:** FY2000 revenue was **$374.5m** [H43].

## 5. CUDA: the long bet (2006–2012)

- **What it is.** CUDA (Compute Unified Device Architecture) is NVIDIA's proprietary platform and API that lets ordinary software run massively parallel work on GPUs. Ian Buck (Brook) and John Nickolls led it. It was **introduced Nov 2006** (FY2026 10-K: "With our introduction of CUDA in 2006…"), and the **public SDK shipped 15–16 Feb 2007** [H44].
- **Cost and scepticism.** NVIDIA put CUDA into every GeForce, which raised costs while gaming customers paid nothing extra for it. Huang, on **Lex Fridman Podcast #494 (23 Mar 2026)**, *as quoted in transcripts*, said the bet consumed the company's gross profit and that "our market cap went down to like one and a half billion dollars" [H45, partial]. Wikipedia says NVIDIA invested "over a billion dollars" in CUDA in the 2000s [H46].
- **CONTESTED, the trough figure.** Market-data sites put NVIDIA's actual low at about **$3.5–4bn (around 20–21 Nov 2008)**. The stock finished 2008 down roughly 76%. Huang's "$1.5bn" does not match 2008 data; it may be a misremembering or refer to another moment. **Narration: "below $4 billion in late 2008" is safe. Huang's $1.5bn needs attribution as his recollection** [H47].

## 6. AlexNet and the DGX-1 (2012–2016)

- **AlexNet, Sept 2012.** Krizhevsky, Sutskever and Hinton submitted it to ImageNet (ILSVRC) on 30 Sep 2012 and won with a 15.3% top-5 error, more than 10 points ahead of the runner-up. It was trained on **two NVIDIA GTX 580 3GB cards** (about $500 each at launch) in **Krizhevsky's bedroom at his parents' house**. The paper explains that the net was split across two GPUs because "A single GTX 580 GPU has only 3GB of memory" [H48]. NVIDIA's own 10-K now calls this "the 'Big Bang' moment of AI" [H49].
- **DGX-1, 2016.** Announced 6 Apr 2016 at $129,000, with 8 Pascal GPUs. In **August 2016 Huang personally delivered the first unit to OpenAI's San Francisco office**. Musk thanked NVIDIA on Twitter on 9 Aug 2016. The lid inscription, per Fortune (15 Aug 2016), began "To Elon and the OpenAI Team!" and continued "to the future of computing and humanity". Huang retold the story on Rogan (Dec 2025). **Partial:** delivery to OpenAI is solid. That Musk *personally* received it rests on photos and Huang's account rather than independent records. "Delivered it to Elon Musk and the OpenAI team" is fair [H54].

## 8. Leadership style, persona, family, wealth

- **Flat org, about 60 direct reports.** Stripe Sessions, Apr 2024, with Patrick Collison, *per transcript excerpts*: "NVIDIA's leadership team is 60 people" and "I'm certain that's the best practice. It's not conventional…" [H59, partial]. Huang told the NYT DealBook Summit (29 Nov 2023, reported by CNBC) that people reporting to him "should be at the top of their game" and "require the least amount of pampering" [H60].
- **No 1:1s.** Widely reported (Fortune, 12 Nov 2024). The cleanest on-record source is Lex Fridman #494 (Mar 2026), where he says one-on-ones with that many reports are impossible (paraphrase) [H61, partial].
- **Top-Five-Things (T5T) emails.** Employees email their top five current priorities and observations to their managers and, often, to Huang. The practice goes back decades. Sources: Kim, *The Nvidia Way*; WSJ via Fortune, 13 Dec 2024; Business Insider [H62, partial].
- **No fixed office; no watch.** He roams conference rooms, and he says he doesn't wear a watch because "now is the most important time" [H63].
- **"Pain and suffering."** **Venue correction:** the famous line came from the **SIEPR Economic Summit at Stanford, March 2024** (Fortune, 13 Mar 2024), not from View From The Top. VFTT (Stanford GSB, interviewer Shantam Jain, Apr 2024) covered the flat org and the toilets. The line: "I wish upon you ample doses of pain and suffering," alongside "People with very high expectations have very low resilience" [H64]. On the **Acquired** podcast (Oct 2023) he said he would not start NVIDIA again knowing "the pain and suffering … the embarrassment and the shame, and the list of all the things that [would] go wrong" (fragmentary, as quoted by Fortune, 23 Oct 2023) [H65].
- **Leather jacket.** A black leather jacket is his signature. In Mar 2024 Zuckerberg swapped jackets with him and posted "He's like Taylor Swift, but for tech". A Huang jacket sold at auction for $960,000 in 2026 (reported) [H66]. He also has an NVIDIA-logo **tattoo**, which he got when the stock hit $100 and first showed at GTC 2010 [H67].
- **Family.** Lori Huang (née Mills), married 1985 [H10]. The children both work at NVIDIA (secondary reporting; partial): **Madison Huang**, senior director in Omniverse/simulation, who joined in 2020 and trained at Le Cordon Bleu; and **Spencer Huang**, robotics product line manager, who joined in 2022 and previously ran a cocktail bar in Taipei [H68]. Pay figures differ by source and proxy year, so don't use them without the proxy. **Lisa Su** (AMD CEO) is a first cousin once removed (Witt p.157) [H69].
- **Net worth: VOLATILE and CONTESTED.** Forbes was about **$141.9bn** in Nov 2026 (per Wikipedia, as of Nov 2026, post-resignation). Bloomberg had about **$182bn** on 19 Aug 2026 (via 24/7 Wall St). Earlier: $3bn (2019) → $90bn (May 2024) → $100bn (Jun 2024). Most of it is NVIDIA stock (he owned about 3.6% per Wikipedia [H58]), so **quote it with "according to Forbes, as of [date]"** [H70].

## 11. Contested or caution list (for the fact-check pass)

- **Initial capital:** $600 (Kim p.43) vs "$40,000 in the bank" (Fortune 2017). **VC round:** $2m (Forbes 2002) vs $20m (Wikipedia text).
- **CUDA trough:** Huang's "$1.5bn" vs market data of about $3.5–4bn in Nov 2008.
- **Sega line:** "six months to live" vs "six months to survive".
- **GeForce 256:** first shipped Aug 1999 (10-K) vs released 11 Oct 1999 (Wikipedia). "First GPU" is NVIDIA's own definition.
- **"Pain and suffering":** SIEPR (Mar 2024), not View From The Top.
- **AMD years:** 1983–85 (10-K, primary) vs 1984–85 (Wikipedia).
- **$1tn:** 30 May 2023 was an *intraday* touch. Say "touched".
- **Musk's personal receipt of the DGX-1:** supported but thin.
- **Net worth:** $141.9bn (Forbes, Nov 2026, via Wikipedia) vs $182bn (Bloomberg, Aug 2026). Always date-stamp it.
- **Scandal facts:** all reported, partly disputed. They belong to the "now" desk and need attribution on air.
