## 1. New leadership

**What happened (primary documents)**
- **8 Nov 2026.** The board opened an independent investigation "in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." It said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised" and "has confidence in Jensen." [F15]
- **10 Nov.** The independent directors hired Joon H. Kim, former acting U.S. Attorney for SDNY, and Wachtell Lipton. [F16]
- **11 Nov.** The board accepted Huang's resignation as President, CEO and director, effective immediately. It was announced on 12 Nov. [F01]
- **17 Nov.** The board created a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, chaired by Suzanne Nora Johnson. [F14]

**Colette M. Kress, interim CEO and still CFO (age 59)** [F01]
- She has been EVP and CFO since September 2013. [F02]
- Before Nvidia: SVP and CFO of Cisco's Business Technology & Operations Finance (2010–13), Microsoft 1997–2010 (CFO of Server & Tools from 2006), and eight years at Texas Instruments. Degrees: BS Finance, University of Arizona; MBA, SMU. [F03]
- Her own framing: she is committed to "building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years." [F73]
- Narrative angle: she is a finance executive, not an engineer, now running the world's most important engineering company. And she is holding both jobs at once.

**Harvey C. Jones, Nvidia's first-ever board chair (age 73)** [F04]
- He has been a director since 1993, so he has sat on the board for the company's entire life.
- He has been Managing Partner of Square Wave Ventures since 2004. He is a former CEO and Executive Chairman of Synopsys. [F04][F05]
- He said: "We expect the company's operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption." [F73]
- On the Q3 call he reportedly said the board "unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." [F10, partial]

**CEO search**
- The board will consider internal and external candidates. [F06]
- Nvidia spokesperson: "We are in the process of selecting a search firm… We have not formally engaged any potential candidates." [F07, partial]
- **Reported:** the FT says the board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas, who spent about seven years at Nvidia before joining Arm. Nvidia called talk of names "premature," and Arm declined to comment. [F08]
- **Speculation only:** media lists also name Lisa Su, Tim Cook, Nikesh Arora, Johny Srouji, Ian Buck and Madison Huang. [F09]
- **Reported:** the FT says Huang has discussed a comeback. Investors Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) and Gavin Baker (Atreides) wrote to the board urging it to consider one, and Trump reportedly backed the idea. The board has shut that door. [F11][F10]

**Other senior leaders (verified roles only)**
- Ajay "Jay" Puri: EVP, Worldwide Field Operations. Joined 2005, EVP since 2009. [F12]
- Debora Shoquist: EVP, Operations. Joined 2007. [F12]
- Tim Teter: EVP and General Counsel. [F12]
- Ian Buck: VP, Hyperscale and HPC, and a key architect of CUDA. He is not an SEC-listed executive officer, so don't call him "CUDA's creator." [F13]

## 2. The first earnings without Huang: Q3 FY27, 17 Nov 2026

**The numbers** [F23][F24]
- Revenue $115.8bn, up 103% y/y.
- Data Center $107.6bn, of which Hyperscale $60.1bn (+71%) and AI Clouds/Industrial/Enterprise $47.5bn (+195%).
- GAAP gross margin 74.8%. GAAP net income $65.3bn.
- Revenue beat the LSEG consensus of $109.1bn. [F28, partial]

**Q4 outlook** [F22]
- Revenue $130bn ±3%. The quarter includes an extra week.
- Gross margin 71–72%.
- **No China data-centre compute revenue assumed.**

**Kress, verbatim from the filed release** [F17]
> "Our momentum is real. NVIDIA is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people… Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater."

**Kress on the call and on CNBC** (reported; *partial*, check against the replay)
- "Business as usual." [F18]
- To Jim Cramer: "customers continue to be with us." Separately reported: customers and suppliers are "100% behind us." [F19]
- On spending: Nvidia will be "rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees" and stay "disciplined in how we commit capital." [F20]
- On China: Q3 shipments of licensed Hopper data-centre products to China were **$2.4bn**. That figure is primary [F21]. Her statement that Nvidia is no longer shipping these products to China comes from call coverage.
- **Do not use yet:** a reported "75% growth" framing for FY2028 appears only in a search summary [F27, unverified].

**What analysts didn't ask**
- Fortune reports that no one on the call asked about Singapore, Megaspeed or the reported SEC probe. [F29]

**Why the scale matters (CFO commentary)**
- Supply commitments are $390bn, up from $279bn. Total future commitments are $518bn. [F25]
- Guarantee exposure is up to $118bn, including up to $105bn of credit support for an SB Energy campus in Ohio leased to OpenAI. [F25]
- Nvidia returned a record $28.1bn to shareholders in the quarter and has $227.3bn of buyback authorization left. [F26]
- Kress inherits all of this.

## 4. China

**Revenue history** (customer-HQ basis; Nvidia changed the basis in Q3 FY26) [F38]

| Fiscal year | China (incl. HK) revenue | Share of total |
|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $12.3bn | |
| FY2025 | $25.0bn | 19.2% |
| FY2026 | $19.7bn | 9.1% |

- On the old billing basis, FY2025 was $17.1bn (~13%). Always say which basis you're using.
- Q2 FY27 China revenue was $7.88bn of $96.2bn on the HQ basis, which includes non-data-centre revenue. [F45]

**Export-control timeline**
- **9 Apr 2025.** The U.S. requires a license for H20 sales to China. [F39]
- **Q1 FY26.** H20 sales were $4.6bn before the rule. Nvidia took a **$4.5bn charge**, couldn't ship another $2.5bn, and absorbed about $8.0bn of lost H20 revenue in its Q2 outlook. [F40]
- **Aug 2025.** Some H20 licenses were granted, but only about $60m was sold. U.S. officials expected a 15%+ cut. [F41]
- **Dec 2025–Jan 2026.** Trump allowed H200 sales for a 25% cut. BIS then set a case-by-case licensing rule, with a 25% tariff on chips inspected in the U.S. [F42]
- **Feb 2026.** A license for small H200 volumes was granted. Nvidia had booked no H200 revenue by the time of the 10-K. [F43]
- **2026 (Q2 FY27 10-Q).** Beijing restricted H200 purchases. Nvidia took a $0.4bn H200 charge, licensed shipments were under 1% of Data Center revenue, and Nvidia couldn't pass the 25% tariff on to customers. [F44]
- **Q3 FY27.** $2.4bn of Hopper shipped to China, then stopped. The Q4 outlook assumes zero China data-centre compute revenue. [F21][F22]

**Bessent's accusation (13–15 Nov)** [F47, partial]
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said "there is a lot of leakage in the system."
- He questioned whether chips reached China through Singapore-based Megaspeed, and whether Nvidia used "seemingly illicit sales" to boost its earnings.
- He called for a "dramatic shakeup" and an outside CEO, and gave the board and its counsel **10 days** to answer, which lands around 25 Nov.
- He said that if Nvidia were a bank, "a lot of the executives would be removed."

**Nvidia's response**
- Nvidia did not respond directly to Bessent. [F48]
- Its standing position is in its filings. In Q2 FY26, $10.2bn (22%) of revenue was billed to Singapore, which Nvidia says is an invoicing hub, and over 99% of controlled data-centre compute billed there was for orders from U.S.-based customers. [F46]
- Its structural answer was the new oversight committee on 17 Nov. [F14]
- **Reported:** the WSJ says the SEC is opening a civil probe into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. [F49]

## 5. Competition and the customer threat

**Merchant rivals**
- **AMD.** Q3 2026 Data Center revenue was a record **$8.5bn** (+97%), on the MI450 launch. That is about one-thirteenth of Nvidia's $107.6bn. [F50]
  - Its OpenAI deal covers 6 GW, starting with 1 GW of MI450 in 2H 2026, plus a warrant for up to 160m AMD shares. [F51]
  - AMD says Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle are running workloads on its first Helios racks. [F52, partial]
- **Broadcom (custom chips).** AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7bn in FQ3 (+221%), guided to $21.7bn in FQ4 (+236%). [F53]
  - It says it has "line of sight" to about $115bn of AI revenue in FY27 and $230bn in FY28. [F54, partial]
  - Its OpenAI deal covers 10 GW of OpenAI-designed chips, deploying from 2H 2026 through 2029. [F55]

**Hyperscaler in-house chips**
- **Google TPU.** Anthropic has a deal for up to 1m TPUs. Meta's TPU use is still *reported talks*, not a confirmed external sale. [F56]
- **Amazon.** Its custom-chip run rate is above $25bn, and it has over $225bn of Trainium commitments. Trainium3 has shipped since early 2026. [F57]
- **Microsoft.** Maia 200 (3nm) was announced in January 2026. The claimed 30% better performance per dollar is a vendor claim. [F58]
- **Meta.** MTIA volumes are not verified. Meta is also expanding its use of Vera Rubin. [F59]

**Why it matters: customer concentration**
- In Q1 FY27, three direct customers were 21%, 17% and 16% of revenue. In Q2, one was 16%. [F61]
- The customers building their own chips are Nvidia's biggest buyers.

**The CUDA moat debate (analyst views)**
- *Bear case:* JPMorgan's Cembalest argues AI-assisted porting has cut moving a model to AMD from 6–12 months to days. He puts Nvidia's share of announced post-2026 compute deals at about 39% (another version says 28%). [F60, unverified]
- *Bull case (Nvidia's own data):* the fastest-growing part of the business is non-hyperscaler customers (neoclouds, enterprise, sovereign), up 195% [F24]. Those buyers are the least likely to build their own chips.
- *Nvidia's hedge:* it is opening its platform to other people's chips. NVLink Fusion connects custom XPUs (e.g. MediaTek) to Nvidia's rack platform, and in Dec 2025 Nvidia took a non-exclusive license to Groq's LPU technology ($14.4bn goodwill). [F62]

## 6. Product roadmap

- **Blackwell Ultra.** Still growing in Q3 and improving gross-margin mix. [F64]
- **Vera Rubin (2026).** Production shipments began in Q3 FY27, "the fastest product ramp in the company's history." Racks are live at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Azure, OCI and Nebius, and supply is constrained. [F63]
- **Rubin Ultra (2027).** Moves to the new Kyber rack (NVL144). Announced at GTC, 16–19 Mar 2026. [F65, partial]
- **Feynman (2028).** Stacked GPU, paired with a new Rosa CPU, an LP40 LPU, BlueField-5 and CX10, with copper and co-packaged optical scale-up. These are announced plans only. [F65, partial]
- **Narrative point:** everything through 2028 was mapped out under Huang. The real test of succession is the generation after Feynman.

## 7. Founder- and legend-exit precedents

| Company | Departure | What happened next |
|---|---|---|
| Apple | Jobs resigned Aug 2011 (Apple ~$350bn) | Under Tim Cook, the operator, it became the first U.S. company worth $1tn on 2 Aug 2018 [F66] |
| Microsoft | Gates handed the CEO role to Ballmer in Jan 2000 | The stock didn't close above its Dec 1999 record until 21 Oct 2016, about 17 years, under Nadella. Gates stayed on as chairman [F67] |
| Intel | Grove stepped down as CEO in 1998 (~$25bn revenue, $6.9bn profit) and stayed chairman to 2004 | The successor era passed on the original iPhone chip. Grove wasn't a founder, so frame this as a "legend exit" [F68] |
| Starbucks | Schultz left as CEO in 2000 | He returned in Jan 2008, slowed openings, closed stores, and shut every U.S. store for a day to retrain staff [F69] |
| Uber | Kalanick was pushed out in June 2017 | The May 2019 IPO priced at $45 and closed day one at $41.57. Uber's first full-year operating profit came in 2023 (~$1.1bn) [F70] |

Pattern for narration: the business usually survives the founder. The real risks are the *next* platform shift (Microsoft, Intel) and the boomerang (Starbucks, and Huang's reported comeback push).

## 8. Scenarios (labelled scenarios, not forecasts)

**A. Continuity: "Apple after Jobs"**
- *Thesis:* the roadmap through 2028 is locked, demand is supply-constrained, and an operator CEO keeps the machine running.
- *Signals to watch:*
  1. Q4 revenue lands at or above the $130bn guide, with gross margin within 71–72%. [F22]
  2. A permanent CEO is named who has a technical background (e.g. a Haas-type hire), with no exodus of executives.
  3. Rubin Ultra stays on its 2027 schedule. [F65]

**B. Erosion by custom chips: "the customers become competitors"**
- *Thesis:* the hyperscalers shift incremental spend to TPU, Trainium, Maia and Broadcom-built chips.
- *Signals to watch:*
  1. Broadcom's AI revenue keeps compounding faster than Nvidia's Hyperscale segment. Hyperscale grew +71% vs Broadcom AI +221%. [F24][F53]
  2. A confirmed external TPU sale to Meta or another third party. [F56]
  3. Gross margin falling below 70%, or the share of revenue from hyperscalers slipping. [F22][F61]

**C. AI-capex downturn: "the cycle turns"**
- *Thesis:* hyperscaler spending plateaus, neocloud financing tightens, and Nvidia's commitments and guarantees become liabilities.
- *Signals to watch:*
  1. 2027 capex guides come in below the ~$935bn Street consensus. [F71]
  2. DSO keeps rising past 64 days and inventory keeps building past $39.7bn. [F72]
  3. Nvidia actually pays out on guarantees ($118bn maximum exposure), or writes down more equity stakes (already a $1.8bn impairment). [F25][F72]

**D. Regulatory and China escalation: "the investigations become the story"**
- *Thesis:* the Megaspeed and Singapore questions, Bessent's demands and a possible SEC case produce penalties, restatements or new export curbs.
- *Signals to watch:*
  1. How the board answers Bessent's 10-day request (~25 Nov). [F47]
  2. Formal confirmation of the SEC probe, or a Commerce action against Megaspeed. [F49]
  3. The China outlook stays at zero, or Nvidia changes its geographic disclosure. [F22][F46]
